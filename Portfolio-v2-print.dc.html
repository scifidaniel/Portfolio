// PIPELINE & TOOLING

Tools that keep the studio from breaking.

Three After Effects tools I designed and shipped while leading the studio. Built with AI-assisted development — the diagnosis and design were mine; AI was the implementation multiplier.

01 — DEEP COLLECT
SHIPPED
Fixing broken file handoffs in After Effects
THE PROBLEM

On a team, everyone opens everyone else's project files. And every single time, After Effects greeted us with a wall of missing-asset errors — sometimes one or two files, sometimes an entire morning lost hunting down and relinking assets one by one before any real work could start.

DIGGING INTO WHY

Instead of relinking by hand forever, I traced it to the root — and found two causes stacked on top of each other:

01

Our shared assets lived on a synced Google Drive, but third-party plugins like Motion Bro quietly stored their assets in local folders on the C: drive. Those never traveled with the file — so they broke on every other machine.

02

And AE's own Collect Files made it worse. It nested assets into deep subfolder trees, pushing file paths past Windows' 260-character limit (MAX_PATH). Past that limit, Windows silently ignores the file — so the tool meant to prevent missing assets was causing them.

THE FIX

I built a collector that flattens everything — every asset, including the ones stranded on local C: drives — into a single folder on the shared drive. Short paths, nothing left behind. And because everything sits together, if a path ever does break, relinking one file auto-relinks the entire project.

IMPACT

A recurring failure that cost up to half a day of relinking dropped to essentially zero. File handoffs across the team just… worked.

02 — MASTER PANEL
SHIPPED
One panel for every repetitive move in a project
THE PROBLEM

Our projects were heavy — often hundreds of layers deep, cut into dozens of scene blocks, built on multicam green-screen footage that needed serious cleanup. And they were full of small, constant frictions. None of these was a disaster on its own, but they happened all day, every day, across the whole team.

01

Parenting a layer to another one a few hundred rows away meant dragging the pick-whip and waiting for the timeline to crawl.

02

Dropping a layer into a spot buried in the middle of the stack was a fight — and every new Epidemic sound effect landed at the top and had to be hauled back into place.

03

Scene blocks with staggered in/out points couldn't be nudged with the usual shortcuts without collapsing their timing and breaking the scene.

MY APPROACH

Instead of living with them, I built a small tool for each one. Over time I realised they were all the same class of problem — friction between me and the timeline — and consolidated them into a single panel the team could reach for.

WHAT IT DOES
Taming a huge timeline
4 MODULES
▸ Parent
Parent to any layer by typing its number — no dragging the pick-whip across hundreds of rows.
▸ Move
Send a layer above any layer number in one click; snap top-of-stack Epidemic SFX exactly where they belong.
▸ Block Moving
Move a whole scene block to the playhead without flattening each layer's in/out points.
▸ Audio dB
Set levels in bulk — presets for the values I reach for most, scoped to selection, label, or marker.
Automating the green-screen pipeline
BESPOKE · 2
▸ Copy Props by Marker
Finish one camera angle, then push its keying, colour, masks & position to every same-marker layer at once.
▸ Insert & Trim by Marker
Drop the right background behind each angle and auto-trim it to match, by marker.
★ The crunch-time saver

Multicam green-screen lecture footage, poorly shot in-camera: every angle needs its own key, grade and mask, so presets are useless — the numbers that fix one clip break the next. I tag each angle with a marker, perfect one layer, and propagate. The time saved here is modest, maybe 10–15 minutes a pass, and that's not the point. Green-screen cleanup is the last thing I touch, after hours of motion and sound are already locked — which is exactly when hand-copying effects across a dozen constantly-switching camera angles goes wrong. This makes that step deterministic: one click per angle, identical every time, no slips at the moment I'm most likely to make them. The real win isn't minutes; it's consistency and peace of mind at crunch time.

IMPACT

The value is cumulative: dozens of small frictions, each a few seconds or a few clicks, taken out of every project, every day, for anyone who opened the panel. And several don't just save time, they remove ways to get it wrong — moving a scene block without breaking its timing, copying properties identically every pass. On their own each is trivial; but together, they change how fast, and how reliably, a heavy project moves from setup to delivery. Because it all lives in one panel the whole team reached for, that saving compounded across everyone, not just me.

03 — PRESET MANAGER
SHIPPED
Browse a whole template pack at a glance, click to place
THE PROBLEM

Downloading a big template pack should save time — but first you have to find the one comp you actually want, and there's no fast way to do it. Open a comp, wait for it to load, hit play, wait for the RAM preview to cache, watch it, go back, open the next.

Across a pack of dozens of comps, just deciding what to use burns half an hour before any real work starts — and you repeat it every time you come back to that pack.

MY APPROACH

Pay the previewing cost once instead of every time. The system comes in two parts: a batch renderer that turns every template comp into a lightweight preview video, and a browser that lays those previews out as a grid of looping thumbnails. Browsing and placing collapse into a single click.

WHAT IT DOES
AUTO RENDER EXTENSION
PASS 1
▸ Batch render
Take any set of template comps, point them at an output folder, and render them all unattended.
▸ Preview setting
A dedicated lightweight preset — previews stay fast to make and fast to play.
PRESET MANAGER
PASS 2
▸ Pack grid
Reads the rendered previews and lays the whole pack out as looping thumbnails, organised by pack.
▸ Click to place
Click a thumbnail and the matching comp drops straight onto the selected layer at the playhead.
★ The problem I hit — and how it fails

Once real packs went through it, previews started showing up blank. I traced it to the comp names: some authors use special characters (#, %, &) in them, and those characters broke the preview file references — so the grid silently rendered black. Handling the failure well mattered as much as the happy path.

INSTEAD OF FAILING QUIETLY, THE TOOL WALKS YOU TO THE FIX
01
Flags the offending files
02
Lists them by name
03
One button opens the exact folder — rename, reload
IMPACT

What used to be a stop-start ritual of open–play–wait–go-back became a glance and a click. And the gain compounds on packs I reuse: render the previews once, and from then on choosing and placing a template is instant.
